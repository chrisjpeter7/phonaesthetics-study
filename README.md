# phonaesthetics-study

Methology: This study was about phonoaesthetics of 3 letter CVC pseudowords. Consonants could either be plosive (p, t, k) or sonorants (m, n, l), with both consonants in a word belonging to the same category. Vowels could either be front (e, i), or back (o, u). Each participant (n=29) rated 16 pseudowords, 4 of each combination of consonant and vowel types (e.g. sonorant consonant + back vowel). Participants were told the words came from an isolated language with a large prescence of short 3-letter words, and then asked to rate the words' attractiveness. Words were rated on a scale of 0-3 where each participant was asked how attractive they thought a certain word sounded. 

This study was conducted in May 2025, and then subsequently reanalyzed using Python in August 2026. The main finding is a statistically significant difference between perception of front vowels and back vowels, with a p-value of <0.001. This preference for front vowels over back vowels was consistent among both consonant categories. Additionally, there was a near significant difference between male and female perceptions of sonorant and plosive consonants, although there was no difference in the mean rating of the types of consonants among the participants as a whole. However, certain consonants were still more favored over others, with this even differing within the categories (e.g. n was prefered over l in both the C1 and C2 positions).
